About this IP checker

PrivacyLink IP is a small, independent, open-source tool with one job: show the public IP addresses this server observes for your connection. No ads, no account, no analytics, no history of who connected.

Both address families get their own row and their own Copy button. The address of the connection that loaded this page is part of the page itself, so it is visible before any script runs.

What is an IP address?

An IP address is the number a network uses to route traffic to and from your connection. On the public internet, every request carries the address of the connection that sent it, which is why this page can show it to you. It is not your name, your account or your exact location.

Your device also has a private address inside your home or office network, such as 192.168.1.20. Websites do not see that private address; your router translates it to the public address shown above.

IPv4 and IPv6: what is the difference?

IPv4 addresses are four numbers separated by dots, such as 203.0.113.42. IPv6 addresses are longer and use hexadecimal groups separated by colons, such as 2001:db8::1. IPv6 exists because the IPv4 address pool is exhausted.

Many connections support both families. A website only sees the family that the connection used, so this page checks the other family with one additional request. If that check fails, it does not prove your provider has no IPv6 — a VPN, firewall or proxy can block it.

Public vs private IP address

The address shown here is public: it is how your connection appears to the internet. A private address, such as 10.0.0.5 or 192.168.1.20, only works inside your own network. Your router uses NAT to share one public address with several devices.

On mobile networks, carrier-grade NAT can put many customers behind one public address. In that case the address you see belongs to the carrier's gateway, not to your phone alone.

Why does my IP address change?

Most home connections use a dynamic address that the provider can change after a router restart, a lease expiry or network maintenance. A VPN, proxy or a switch between Wi-Fi and mobile data also changes the address a website sees.

IPv6 privacy extensions can rotate the address more often. Each visit to this page is a snapshot of the connection at that moment, not a permanent identifier.

How can I hide my IP address?

A VPN routes your traffic through a server, and websites see that server's address instead of your own. A proxy works similarly, often for a single application. Tor adds more layers at the cost of speed.

Hiding the address does not make you anonymous: cookies, browser fingerprints and logged-in accounts can still identify you. This page does not use them, but other sites may.

Frequently asked questions

What is my IP address?

It is the public address shown at the top of this page: the address this server observes for the connection that loaded the page. If you use a VPN, proxy or carrier-grade NAT, you see that gateway's address. Your device's private address, such as 192.168.1.20, is not visible to websites.

Why do IPv4 and IPv6 show different results?

They are separate address families and can take different routes. A connection may have IPv4 only, IPv6 only or both. IPv6 privacy extensions can also rotate the address between requests, so the value is a snapshot rather than a fixed identity.

Is my IP address personal data?

Under the GDPR, an IP address can be personal data when it can be linked to a person, for example through a provider's records. This service does not build that link: it does not store a history of visitor addresses, set cookies or run analytics.

Can someone find my home with my IP address?

Not the exact address. An IP address can support a rough location estimate, usually a country or region and sometimes a city, but it does not contain your street, name or phone number. Only your provider can map the address to a subscriber.

Does this site store my IP address?

No. The address is used to answer the request and is not written to application logs or a database. The page has no cookies, no analytics and no third-party scripts. Network providers and hosting infrastructure may still process connection metadata.

How do I check my IP address with curl?

Run curl -sS https://{{MAIN_HOST}}/api/ip for plain text, or use /api/ip.json for JSON. The v4. and v6. hostnames force one address family. No key or account is needed.

Why does the IPv6 row say "not reachable"?

The browser could not reach the IPv6-only hostname. That can happen on an IPv4-only connection, but also because of a VPN, firewall, DNS64 or browser policy. A failed check does not prove that your provider has no IPv6.
